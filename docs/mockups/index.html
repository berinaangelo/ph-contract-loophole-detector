HIGHSelf-help eviction / lockout clause
"In case of default, the Landlord may enter the premises, change the locks, and remove the Tenant's belongings without need of a court order."
A landlord cannot lawfully retake possession by self-help — locking out a tenant or removing belongings without a court order. Eviction requires a judicial ejectment (unlawful detainer) action even if the tenant is in default.
Civil Code, Art. 536; Rules of Court, Rule 70 Do not sign as-is; request this clause be removed or revised to reference the judicial eviction process.
HIGHAdvance rent exceeds Rent Control Act cap
"The Tenant shall pay six (6) months advance rent upon signing this Contract of Lease."
This clause requires 6 months advance rent. For units covered by the Rent Control Act, advance rent is capped at 1 month.
RA 9653 (Rent Control Act), Sec. 5 Confirm whether this unit is rent-control covered; if so, renegotiate this term.
Medium severity
MEDIUMRent increase tied to undefined "market rate"
"Upon renewal, the monthly rent shall be adjusted to the prevailing market rate as determined by the Lessor."
Legal for units outside rent-control coverage, but gives the tenant no defined formula or ceiling to budget for or dispute the new rent.
Civil Code, Arts. 19, 1306 Ask for a defined formula or percentage cap instead of an open-ended "market rate."
MEDIUMAny minor violation triggers immediate termination
"Any violation of this Contract by the Tenant, however minor, shall be grounds for immediate termination without prior notice."
Legal to write, but leaves no chance to fix an honest mistake before losing housing — worth negotiating down to a defined cure period.
Civil Code, Arts. 19, 24, 1306 Negotiate a notice-and-cure period (e.g. 5-7 days) before termination applies.
Low severity
LOWFlat utility fee with no link to consumption
"Tenant shall pay a fixed monthly utility fee of Php 1,500 in addition to rent."
Legal to write, but removes the tenant's ability to verify they're being charged fairly against actual usage.
Civil Code, Arts. 19, 1306 Ask whether this can be tied to metered consumption instead of a flat fee.
